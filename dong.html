A minority of China

Dong

Section by Lilo Ryan

Introduction

The Dong people, like the Miao, primarily live in the Guizhou province. Unlike the Miao, however, their population is much smaller. Their total population is around three million. They have a rich cultural tradition of embroidery and other art. They are famed for their architecture and their bridges, as many of the villages are built alongside rivers.

Objects

New piece

Dong Ritual Wear

Description

This Dong ritual wear combines silver, blue, and gold thread in spiraling patterns. There are feathers hung off the belts. This piece is from the Qing Dynasty and from the Guizhou province. One hypothesis about the repeated swirling patterns is that it is connected to the ancestor group of the Dong’s spiritual association with snakes (Guan). The motifs repeat throughout all of the different pieces visible in the images.

Analysis

The intricate costume is intended to be worn only for the Dong minority group Lusheng dance. This festival centers on the Dong Lusheng instrument, which is a key component of the minority group as a whole; it is a bamboo mouth-organ and has many similar iterations in different cultures. The ritual attire seen here has both function (it is meant to be worn) as well as clear spiritual value. Its ornate nature indicates a degree of reverence in the production and time taken to produce a beautiful object.
