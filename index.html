Functional and Religious Art of Minority Groups in China

A digital catalog of objects from the Miao, Dong, Tibetan, Qiang, and Mongol communities — pieces drawn from the Ruth Chandler Williamson Gallery at Scripps College and the Shanghai Museum’s Chinese Minority Nationalities’ Art Gallery, where the line between everyday function and sacred meaning rarely holds still.

Curated by Zhirui Lin · Dominic Jackman · Lilo Ryan
About this project

Why these objects, why now

The objects gathered in this catalog were never made to sit behind glass. A baby carrier was made to carry a baby. A woodblock was made to print a sutra. A headdress was made to be worn, weighed, and seen.

Yet each piece also performs spiritual work. The Miao baby carrier holds a butterfly mother whose wings curve around an infant’s back. The Tibetan woodblock turns a tool of reproduction into a vehicle for the most sacred mantra in Tibetan Buddhism. The Qiang ritual altar marks, in stone, where a community has come from and where it stands.

We have organized this site around five communities — Miao, Dong, Tibetan, Qiang, and Mongol — whose visual traditions complicate any easy separation between the religious and the functional, the sacred and the daily, the “border” and the “center.” Read it as a small reminder that the cultural life of China has always been made at its edges as much as at its core.
