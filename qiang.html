A minority of China

Qiang

Section by Zhirui Lin and Dominic Jackman

Introduction

The Qiang are a small ethnic minority concentrated in the mountainous regions of northwest Sichuan, especially in the Aba and Mianyang prefectures. Their religious life centers on the worship of Mubita and the white-stone deities, mediated by the shibi — ritual shamans who serve as priests, healers, and oral historians. The pieces below trace this religious world: the household altar where Qiang spirituality is enacted, and the figure of the shibi himself.

Objects

Existing piece

Qiang Ritual Altar

By Zhirui Lin

Description

A household ritual altar typically functions as a worship shrine for the supreme god Mubita and for the white-stone deities revered as guardians of heaven, earth, mountains, and trees. Consecrated white stones, which are often taken from sacred snow-capped mountains, sit at the altar’s center, alongside offerings and the ritual instruments and pictographic scriptures (shualeri) that the shibi, the Qiang shaman, carries and unfolds during ceremonies (Zhang).

Analysis

For the Qiang, the ritual altar is not merely a container for sacred objects but an enduring landmark of communal identity. As Yanshuo Zhang documents in Creative Belonging, Qiang elders relocated to modern apartments after the 2008 Wenchuan earthquake insisted on rebuilding worship shrines for Mubita in their new neighborhoods, since “the worship shrine for Mubita serves as a tangible landmark for the Qiang identity and cements the togetherness of the villagers in diaspora” (Zhang). The altar is also a symbol in a wider ritual ecology: the shibi, described by Zhang as “an immensely important figure in transmitting medicinal and social knowledge and in healing, blessing, and exorcising for the Qiang communities,” uses the altar as the locus of weddings, funerals, and exorcism rituals. The 2008 Wenchuan earthquake, which destroyed many vernacular Qiang villages and “killed many aged shibi,” made the safeguarding of these altars and the spiritual knowledge extremely important.

Existing piece

Shibi

By Dominic Jackman

Shibi are ritual shamans in the Qiang culture. They are an integral part of qiang communities, and are key for the preservation of Qiangf myths and traditions. They may organize cermonies, chant, communicate with psirits and heal others. The role of a Shibi involves that of a priest, alongside folk rap, singing, and dance performances, amongst others. As a result, Qiang people have endowed them with a sacred status, believing that they possess a psychic power. Qiang also believes nature to be sacred, and all things are also believed to have an animated quality. As a result, shibi behave as shamans who are able to connect the divine and the earthly with their power to speak with the Gods. However, the roles and responsibilities of shibi are growing as times change. For example, they are asked to “send the red”, the act of putting a red piece of cloth behind the windshield of villagers’ new cars. (Zhang)
